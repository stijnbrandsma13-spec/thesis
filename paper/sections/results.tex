\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}
We organise the discussion around three target parameters: the per-support-point probabilities $\theta_r$ in \Cref{tab:coverage}, the population mean $\mathbb{E}[\beta_1]$ in \Cref{tab:coverage_Eb1}, and $\mathbb{E}[\beta_2]$ in \Cref{tab:coverage_Eb2}.
For each target we report empirical coverage under three constructions: the original FKRB interval, specified in \cref{eq:CIFKRB,eq:CIfunctional}, the sieve Wald interval, specified in \cref{eq:CIChenPouzo}, and the studentized sieve QLR interval, specified in \cref{eq:CIQLR}.
All cells in these tables would, under correct coverage, be exactly $1-\alpha$.

The overall picture is that all three intervals over-cover, often by a large margin.
The FKRB interval is the most conservative throughout: at $\alpha=0.05$ its coverage for $\theta_r$ never falls below $0.994$, and in roughly half of the $\alpha=0.05$ cells it equals $1.000$.
Sieve Wald tracks FKRB almost exactly; the two never differ by more than one thousandth in any cell we report.
This is consistent with the theoretical equivalence in \Cref{sec:FKRBconfidence}: in the linear, identity-weighted sieve specialization the sieve variance (see \cref{eq:CPsievevariance}) reduces to the FKRB delta-method variance (see \cref{eq:FKRBdeltamethod}), so the two constructions only differ by whether they evaluate at the constrained or unconstrained fit and by the post-hoc feasible-range clip.
At interior coordinates these differences vanish and only show up in finite samples through a handful of thousandth-level perturbations.

The sieve QLR interval is markedly less conservative without ever falling materially below nominal at the aggregate.
At $\alpha=0.05$ its coverage for $\theta_r$ sits in the range $[0.953,\, 0.991]$ across all 20 cells; at $\alpha=0.5$ it ranges from $0.541$ in the least challenging cell (wide normal DGP, $N=10{,}000$, $R=25$) up to $0.871$ at $R=225$.
However we should note that the QLR interval does sometimes slightly fall below nominal at individual points that lie deep in the interior.\footnote{The figures that support this were not timely available because of the simulation. These will be added in the final version.}
This does not only increase the probability of type I errors at that points, which is undesirable, but it also distorts the aggregate results presented in \cref{tab:coverage}.

Three qualitative patterns are visible across all three tables.
First, increasing the grid size from $R=25$ to $R=225$ uniformly increases conservativeness, with virtually every FKRB and Wald cell at $R=225$ collapsing to $1.000$.
This is consistent with the boundary explanation: a finer grid puts more support points close to zero mass, where the asymptotic-normality argument underlying \cref{eq:CIOLS} and \cref{eq:CIChenPouzo} fails \citep{Andrews1999}.
QLR coverage also rises with $R$, but more gently: even at $R=225$ it stays in the $[0.75, 0.88]$ band at $\alpha=0.5$ rather than saturating at $1$.
Second, increasing $N$ from $1{,}000$ to $10{,}000$ pushes all three intervals towards nominal at $R=25$ but leaves $R=225$ essentially unchanged, reinforcing that the boundary problem -- rather than first-order asymptotics -- is what is binding at the fine grid.
Third, the ordering of DGPs by conservativeness is broadly the same across methods and targets: the almost single type is the most over-covered, while the wide normal DGP is the least over-covered and is also the only DGP for which the QLR interval ever approaches its nominal level (e.g.\ $0.541$ at $\alpha=0.5$).
This ordering is consistent with the boundary intuition of \Cref{sec:dgp}: in the almost single type DGP almost all probability mass sits on the boundary $\theta_r=0$, whereas in the wide normal DGP mass is spread more uniformly so that comparatively fewer coordinates are pinned to the boundary.

Comparing \Cref{tab:coverage} with the mean-functional tables \Cref{tab:coverage_Eb1,tab:coverage_Eb2} shows that inference on the linear functionals $\mathbb{E}[\beta_1]$ and $\mathbb{E}[\beta_2]$ is uniformly more conservative than on the individual $\theta_r$.
Almost every $\alpha=0.05$ cell in the two mean tables equals $1.000$ exactly for both FKRB and Wald, and the only meaningful variation in those columns appears at $\alpha=0.5$ with $R=25$.
The QLR interval is again notably less conservative, in particular at $N=10{,}000$, $R=25$ and $\alpha=0.5$ it gets close to nominal for the wide normal DGP, but it too remains on the conservative side throughout.

Taken together, the simulations are consistent with the theoretical message of \Cref{sec:Inference}.
At interior coordinates the FKRB and Wald intervals are numerically indistinguishable, both are heavily over-conservative, and switching to the studentized QLR construction recovers a non-trivial amount of length without inducing material under-coverage.
The remaining gap to nominal, particularly at $R=225$, is attributable to the boundary issue that lies outside the regularity conditions of \citet{ChenPouzo2015}.
